About the practice

Osteopathy is hands-on treatment for the muscles, joints and the way your body moves. My focus is on finding what's actually causing a problem, which is often not the place that hurts, and treating that. Sessions involve a mix of soft tissue work, joint articulation and mobilisation, along with straightforward advice on what to do between appointments.

Every appointment is 45 minutes, one to one. I keep the practice small on purpose: no room full of treatment tables, no being passed between practitioners. You see me each time, and I'd rather get you better in a few sessions than keep you coming back indefinitely.

In the UK, "osteopath" is a protected title. All practising osteopaths are registered with the General Osteopathic Council and hold full insurance.

What I treat

The most common things people come in with:

  • Low back pain, including sudden episodes
  • Neck pain and stiffness
  • Shoulder, hip and knee problems
  • Sports injuries and overuse strains
  • Tension and headaches from desk work
  • Sciatica and referred pain
  • Recovery after injury or a long layoff
  • Postural aches and general stiffness

If I don't think osteopathy is the right thing for you, I'll say so at the first appointment and point you towards whoever is better placed to help.

Your first visit

We start by talking: your symptoms, your health history, your work and training, and what you want to get back to. Then a physical assessment, looking at how you move and where things are restricted. Most of the first appointment still leaves plenty of time for treatment.

Before you leave, I'll explain what I think is going on in plain English, what you can do yourself, and roughly how many sessions it's likely to take. Many problems improve noticeably within two to four visits.

Wear something comfortable you can move in. Leggings, shorts or loose trousers are ideal. You'll only ever be asked to undress to the level you're comfortable with, and you don't need a GP referral to book.

Common questions

Does treatment hurt?

Some techniques can feel tender while they're happening, especially around already-sore areas, but treatment shouldn't be painful. I'll check in as we go and adjust to what you're comfortable with.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on the problem and how long you've had it. Many things improve within two to four sessions. You'll get an honest estimate at the first appointment, and I won't book you in for more than you need.

Can I claim on health insurance?

Most private policies cover osteopathy. Check your policy first, and I'll provide receipts for your claim.

What if I need to cancel?

Life happens. Just give as much notice as you can through the booking system or by phone so the slot can go to someone else.
